# Capital Allocation and Financial Markets (Chapter 2)

- Capital allocation: transfer of money from those with surplus (savers/investors) to those with need (borrowers)
  - Efficient market ideal: instant matching at zero cost, purely theoretical
- Three transfer methods:
  1. Direct transfer: borrower gives equity/ownership directly to investor
  2. Indirect via investment bank (IB): IB buys securities from issuer, resells to retail investors at a markup to compensate for inventory risk
  3. Indirect via financial intermediary (bank): bank takes deposits at low rates (e.g. 0.1%) and lends at higher rates (e.g. 7%); the spread is the bank's profit
- Key distinction: IB passes through the same security; banks redesign the security (deposit vs. loan)
- Five financial market pairs:
  1. Physical vs. financial asset markets: tangible goods vs. stocks, bonds, derivatives
  2. Spot vs. futures markets: settlement within days vs. months/years out; futures used to hedge price risk
  3. Money vs. capital markets: securities maturing within 1 year (T-bills, commercial paper, CDs) vs. over 1 year (mortgages, bonds)
  4. Primary vs. secondary markets: newly issued stocks vs. existing stocks trading between investors
  5. Private vs. public markets: customized contracts (e.g. business loans) vs. standardized securities (e.g. stocks)
- Derivatives overview:
  - Call option: right to buy a stock at a predetermined strike price; profitable when price rises above strike
  - Put option: right to sell at a predetermined strike price; used with short strategy when price expected to fall
  - Short selling: borrow shares, sell high, buy back lower, return shares, pocket the difference

# Commercial Paper In-Class Exercise

- Commercial paper: short-term debt issued by large corporations to institutional investors (not banks)
  - Typical maturity: 30 days; rates regulated by the Fed
  - AA-rated non-financial firms (e.g. Tesla): 3.68% annual rate for 30-day paper (2026 data)
- Bonus point problem: Tesla borrows $10M for 30 days at 3.68% annual rate
  - Correct method: $10M × 0.0368 ÷ 365 × 30 = ~$30,247 in interest
  - Common wrong answers: dividing by 12 (monthly) or not adjusting for days at all

# IPOs, SPACs, and Secondary Markets

- IPO surge in 2021 (1,035 IPOs): backlog from COVID delays, not a sudden spike in new businesses
- SPACs (Special Purpose Acquisition Companies):
  - Shell company goes public at $10/share with no underlying business
  - Raises capital, then searches for a private company to acquire within 12-24 months
  - Target company becomes public through the merger, bypassing traditional IPO scrutiny
  - Investors bet on the SPAC sponsor's track record, not the eventual business
  - Less regulatory burden than IPO: no financials to file until acquisition closes
  - Risk: high uncertainty, no operating history to evaluate
