## Summary

# Market Value Added (MVA)

- MVA = Market Capitalization minus Shareholders’ Equity
  - Market cap = share price x shares outstanding
  - Example: $10/share x 75M shares = $750M market cap
  - Shareholders’ equity = $940M, so MVA = negative $190M
- Negative MVA: market value below book value, signals low investor confidence
- Higher MVA = better; reflects management’s ability to boost stock price
- Limitation: rising markets inflate all stock prices regardless of management quality

# Economic Value Added (EVA)

- EVA = NOPAT minus Cost of Capital
  - NOPAT = EBIT x (1 minus tax rate)
  - EBIT found on the income statement
  - Cost of Capital = after-tax cost of capital % x total invested capital
- Example: EBIT $278M, tax rate 25%, capital $1M at 10% cost
  - EVA = $208.4M (positive: value created exceeds borrowing cost)
- Positive EVA: benefits outweigh cost of capital, efficient use of borrowed funds
- Negative EVA: cost outweighs benefit, borrowing destroys firm value

# EVA vs. Net Income vs. MVA

- Net income only accounts for debt financing cost (interest payments)
- EVA uses WACC, capturing both debt and equity financing costs
  - More comprehensive measure of true borrowing cost
- MVA measures only whole-company performance (tied to stock price)
- EVA can measure divisional or departmental performance
  - Stock price cannot be attributed to individual teams or divisions

# Federal and State Income Tax

- Progressive tax system: higher income = higher rate
- Federal tax calculation:
  - Find the correct income bracket
  - Tax = base amount + marginal rate x (income minus bracket lower bound)
  - Common mistake: applying marginal rate to total income, not the excess above threshold
- Example: $87,000 earner (third federal bracket)
  - Federal tax: $14,447; state (CT) tax: \~$4,000; total: \~$18,000
  - Average federal tax rate: 16.6% (federal tax / annual income)
- Example: couple earning $250,000
  - Federal bracket: over $231,250, not over $578,125
  - Federal tax: $59,394.50; average federal rate: 23.76%
  - State (CT) bracket: over $200,000, up to $250,000
  - State tax: $11,050 + 6.5% x ($250,000 minus $200,000); average state rate: 5.72%
  - Combined average: \~30 cents per dollar earned

# Key Takeaways

- Know how to calculate MVA and EVA, and where to source each input
- Interpret sign and magnitude: higher is better for both; negative signals value destruction
- Understand EVA’s advantages over net income (WACC) and MVA (divisional measurement)
- For tax problems: always apply marginal rate to income above the bracket threshold, not total income
