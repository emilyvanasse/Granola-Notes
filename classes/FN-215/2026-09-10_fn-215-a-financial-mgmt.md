# Chapter 1 Review: Business Structures

- Three forms: proprietorship (one owner), partnership (multiple owners), corporation (legal entity)
- Corporation's four key features:
  1. Legal entity established by the state
  2. Ownership and management are separate
  3. Unlimited business longevity
  4. Easy transferability of ownership (+ limited liability: max loss = amount invested)
- Advantages of proprietorship/partnership: easy to form, fewer regulations
- Disadvantages: unlimited personal liability, limited business life, hard to raise large capital
- Corporate disadvantages: double taxation (corporate income tax + personal income tax on dividends), heavy government regulation

# Intrinsic Value vs. Stock Price

- Ultimate managerial goal: maximize intrinsic value (not stock price)
  - Stock price used as a proxy since intrinsic value cannot be directly observed
  - In equilibrium, stock price = intrinsic value; but frictions (fees, time lags) mean markets are rarely in equilibrium
- Determinants of intrinsic value: true cash flow and true risk
  - Higher cash flow raises firm value; higher risk lowers it
- Determinants of stock price: perceived cash flow and perceived risk, driven by demand and supply
  - Marginal investors (large groups of retail investors with shared beliefs) set market price
- Key distinctions:
  - Intrinsic value is an estimate; different analysts reach different conclusions
  - Stock price fluctuates based on market perception, not complete information
  - Managers make better intrinsic value estimates (more internal information); outside analysts should still verify

# Conflicts of Interest

- Agency problem: conflict between shareholders (principals) and managers (agents)
  - Managers tend to maximize personal wealth; shareholders want long-term firm value
  - Three solutions:
    1. Compensation packages mixing cash, stock, and options (aligns manager wealth with firm performance)
    2. Lower barriers for retail investor participation (threshold reduced to $1,000 held for 1+ year to attend annual shareholder meetings)
    3. Hostile takeover threat (CEOs risk losing jobs if firm becomes an acquisition target)
- Bondholder vs. stockholder conflict
  - Bondholders receive fixed income; stockholders get variable returns with no cap
  - Stockholders prefer riskier projects (higher upside); bondholders do not
  - Two solutions:
    1. Debt covenants (e.g., maintain minimum equity ratio of 30%)
    2. Bondholders demand higher interest rates to compensate for risk
- CEO compensation structure (S&P 1500 data):
  - Average total compensation ~$8M; stock awards ~$3.2M (~37.5% of total)
  - Stock is the largest single component, ahead of salary, bonus, and options
