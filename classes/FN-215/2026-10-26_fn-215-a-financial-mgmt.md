# Single Cash Flow Review

- Future value (compounding): FV = PV × (1 + r/m)^(n×m)
  - r = annual rate, m = compounding frequency, n = years
- Present value (discounting): PV = FV / (1 + r/m)^(n×m)
  - PV at any point in time: discount only the remaining periods
- Excel functions: FV, PV, NPER, RATE

# Annuities: Ordinary vs. Annuity Due

- Ordinary annuity: equal cash flows at end of each period
  - Type = 0 (default in Excel)
- Annuity due: cash flows at beginning of each period
  - Type = 1; requires smaller deposits or shorter time to reach same goal
  - Earlier deposits earn more compounding periods
- FV and PV of annuity: use FV/PV with PMT; no present/future value input unless specified

# Irregular Cash Flows and Bond Pricing

- Non-annuity cash flows: discount each individually, then sum
  - Example: NVIDIA dividends of $4, $10, $8 over 3 years at 5% = $19.79 intrinsic value
  - Compare intrinsic value to market price to decide buy/sell
- Bond pricing: split last payment ($50 coupon + $1,000 principal) into two parts
  - Annuity: PV of 10 × $50 at 5% = $386.09
  - Single cash flow: PV of $1,000 at year 10 = $613.91
  - Total = $1,000 (coupon rate = discount rate → priced at par)
- Intrinsic value vs. market price
  - Market price < intrinsic value: undervalued, buy
  - Market price > intrinsic value: overvalued, do not buy

# APR vs. EAR and Upcoming Deadlines

- APR: stated annual rate, ignores compounding frequency
- EAR: effective annual rate, accounts for compounding
  - Formula: EAR = (1 + APR/m)^m − 1
  - Example: 16% APR compounded monthly → EAR = 17.22%
  - High APR cards (e.g., 23%) can yield EAR of \~27–30%
