# AI Investment and Data Centers

- Companies investing in AI too quickly, without clear long-term productivity or economic growth projections
- US has roughly 5,200 data centers currently
- Target towns for new data centers: rural, low-income, weak political power, existing cable infrastructure, near water (for cooling)
  - Example: Maysville, KY (pop. under 10,000, median household income \~$34K–$42K)
  - Meta offered $26M for a 1,200-acre farm there; farmer refused (family land for 3 generations)
  - Pennsylvania town offered $10,000 cash per household just to approve construction
- Downsides of data center expansion: higher local electricity demand and prices, water pollution from cooling chemicals
- Connecticut alone has at least 52 data centers mapped

# Accounting Standards and Regulatory Bodies

- US firms follow GAAP (Generally Accepted Accounting Principles)
  - FASB (Financial Accounting Standards Board) issues and revises GAAP
  - SEC has final authority to enforce accounting standards
- International firms follow IFRS (International Financial Reporting Standards)
  - IASB (International Accounting Standards Board) is the standard-setting body
- SOX Act (2002, post-Enron): CEO and CFO personally responsible for accuracy of financial statements
  - Enron cooked financials by underreporting expenses to inflate profits while cash-insolvent

- Net Working Capital (NWC) = current assets minus current liabilities
  - 2024: $4,233M – $13,486M = **–$9,253M** (negative = weak liquidity)
  - Negative NWC means insufficient liquid assets to cover immediate obligations
- Net Operating Working Capital (NOWC) = operating current assets minus operating current liabilities
  - Excludes financing-related items (e.g. current portion of long-term debt)
  - 2024: $4,233M – ($13,486M – $1,799M) = **–$7,454M**
  - 2025: $5,144M – ($13,306M – $750M) = **–$7,412M**
  - Two years needed to calculate change in NOWC for free cash flow

# Income Statement Structure

- Revenue (top line)
- Minus operating costs (salaries, materials, operational expenses) → **EBIT** (Earnings Before Interest and Taxes)
- Minus interest payments → **EBT** (Earnings Before Taxes)
- Minus taxes → **Net income** (bottom line, the profit)
