## Summary

# Responsibility Centers and Performance Reports

- Four responsibility center types: cost, revenue, profit, investment
  - Investment center = company as a whole (e.g., Frito-Lay division)
  - Org chart example: PepsiCo with VPs by geography and product, managers per brand
- Performance reports compare actual vs. budgeted revenues and expenses
- Variance = difference between actual and budget
  - Revenue: favorable when actual > budget
  - Costs: favorable when actual < budget
- Management by exception: only investigating large variances is discouraged
  - Excel makes reviewing all variances practical; don’t ignore small ones

# Segment Margin and Investment Center Formulas

- Segment margin format: Sales → minus variable costs → contribution margin → minus direct fixed costs → segment margin → minus common fixed costs → operating income
  - Direct fixed costs: traceable to the profit center
  - Common fixed costs: allocated across divisions
- Four investment center performance measures (formula sheet provided on exam, slide 25):
  - ROI: operating income ÷ total assets
  - Sales margin: operating income ÷ sales
  - Capital turnover: sales ÷ total assets
  - Residual income: operating income minus (target rate of return × total assets)
- PepsiCo example: Snack Division ROI 61.2% vs. Beverage Division 5.2%
  - Snack: $5,340 income, $8,730 assets; Beverage: $1,937 income, $37,039 assets
  - Higher snack ROI doesn’t mean abandoning beverages; beverages support snack sales
- Residual income often leads to better goal congruence than ROI alone

# Flexible Budgets and Volume Variance

- Comprehensive budget = apples-to-oranges when actual volume differs from budgeted volume
- Flexible budget rebases costs to actual volume for apples-to-apples comparison
  - Fixed expenses stay the same regardless of volume; variances there are worth investigating
- Volume variance: flexible budget vs. comprehensive budget
- Flexible budget variance: actual vs. flexible budget
- Transfer pricing (internal sales between divisions):
  - Three methods: market price, negotiated price, cost-plus markup
  - Selling division wants highest price; buying division wants lowest
  - Global considerations: customs, duties, and varying tax rates affect pricing

# Balanced Scorecard and Class Exercises

- Balanced scorecard: financial + non-financial KPIs across four perspectives
  - Financial: revenues, expenses, productivity
  - Customer: price, quality, service, delivery
  - Internal business: innovation, operations, post-sale service
  - Learning and growth: employee development and morale
- Exercise 21 (McCall Sports, cost center performance report):
  - Variance % = variance ÷ budgeted amount
  - Indirect labor: $4,200 unfavorable (16.8%); repairs and maintenance: $1,215 favorable (16.2%)
  - Both flagged for investigation at the 15% threshold; investigate all variances, not just unfavorable

  - Actual segment margin $447,253 vs. budgeted $367,200: favorable variance of $80,053
