## Summary

### CVP Fundamentals

- Contribution margin per unit: selling price minus variable cost
  - Kay’s Posters: $35 price, $21 variable cost, $14 contribution margin
  - Contribution margin ratio: $14 / $35 = 40%
- Contribution margin income statement: sales, minus variable costs, equals contribution margin, minus fixed costs, equals operating income
  - Each additional poster sold adds $14 to operating income
- 60 cents of each sales dollar covers variable costs; 40 cents contributes to fixed costs and profit (Exhibit 7-4, p. 447)

### Break-Even and Target Profit Calculations

- Three approaches (shortcut preferred):
  1. Income statement approach
  2. Contribution margin per unit: fixed costs / CM per unit = units needed
  3. Contribution margin ratio: fixed costs / CM ratio = sales dollars needed
- Kay’s break-even: $7,000 / $14 = 500 posters, or $7,000 / 40% = $17,500 in sales
- Target profit formula: (fixed costs + target profit) / CM per unit
  - Kay targets $4,900 profit: ($7,000 + $4,900) / $14 = 850 posters, or $29,750 in sales
- Socks Unlimited (Exercise 24):
  - $2 price, $1.20 variable cost, $0.80 CM, 40% CM ratio, $85,000 fixed costs
  - Break-even: 106,250 packages, or $212,500 in sales
  - Target profit of $22,000: 133,750 packages

### Sensitivity Analysis and Sales Mix

- Price changes: lower price reduces CM, raises break-even volume; higher price does the opposite
  - Kay at $31: CM drops to $10, break-even rises from 500 to 700 posters
  - Selling below variable cost ($20 \< $21) always a loss; positive CM is essential
- Variable cost changes: supplier increase to $24 raises break-even from 500 to 637 posters
- Fixed cost changes: moving to cheaper space cuts fixed costs from $7,000 to $3,190, break-even drops to 290 posters (with $11 CM)
- Sales mix: weighted average CM used when selling multiple products
  - Regular poster: $14 CM; large poster: $30 CM
  - Mix ratio: 5 regular, 3 large per 8 sold
  - Weighted average CM: ($14×5 + $30×3) / 8 = $20
  - Break-even: $7,000 / $20 = 350 posters (219 regular, 131 large)

### Margin of Safety and Operating Leverage

- Margin of safety: actual/expected sales minus break-even sales (p. 469)
  - Kay example: 950 expected, 500 break-even = 450-poster cushion
  - In dollars: 450 × $35 = $15,750
  - As a percentage: 450 / 950 = 47.4%
- Operating leverage: ratio of fixed to variable costs in total cost structure (p. 471)
  - High operating leverage: high fixed costs, low variable costs, high CM ratio, higher risk and higher return
    - Examples: golf courses, hotels, airlines, theme parks
  - Low operating leverage: low fixed costs, high variable costs
