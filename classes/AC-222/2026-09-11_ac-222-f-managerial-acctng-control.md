## Summary

### Job Costing vs. Process Costing

- Process costing: mass production of identical units (soup, toothpaste, soft drinks)
  - Averages manufacturing costs across all units produced
  - Not covered in detail this semester
- Job costing: unique, custom, or small-batch products and services
  - Examples: Boeing, custom furniture, law firms, accounting firms, web design, medical practices
  - Cost object is the job; outputs are single units or small batches
- Key distinction: service-based businesses default to job costing; homogeneous mass production = process costing

### Production Flow and Supporting Documents

- Flow: Raw Materials → Work in Process → Finished Goods → Cost of Goods Sold
- Bill of materials: lists all raw materials needed per job (quantities and part numbers)
- Raw materials record: tracks units received, requisitioned, and remaining (internal control)
- Purchase order + receiving report + invoice must all match before paying a supplier
- Job cost record: tracks direct materials, direct labor, and overhead per job
  - Shows units produced and cost per unit
  - Template usable for class team project

### Predetermined Overhead Rate

- Formula: Total Estimated Overhead Cost ÷ Total Estimated Allocation Base = Overhead Rate
  - Numerator and denominator are always provided in the problem
  - Allocation base is typically direct labor hours (confirmed per problem)
- Overhead is an estimate; actual amounts are reconciled later
- Indirect materials are overhead but were omitted from the publisher's slide (noted by instructor)
- Under-allocated: actual overhead > allocated (job was under-costed → increase COGS)
- Over-allocated: allocated overhead > actual (job was over-costed → decrease COGS)
- Journal entries not required; accounting majors can reference Objective 6 in the textbook

### Worked Examples

- Exercise 23 (Playtime Industries):
  - Overhead rate: $586,600 ÷ 41,900 hrs = $14/hr
  - Job 304: $14,800 materials + $7,220 labor (190 hrs × $38) + $2,660 overhead (190 × $14) = $24,680
  - Selling price at 28% markup: $24,680 × 1.28 = $31,590
- Elkland Heating and Cooling:
  - Overhead rate: $61,500 ÷ 4,100 hrs = $15/hr
  - Job 101: $15,000 materials + $3,720 labor (155 × $24) + $2,325 overhead (155 × $15) = $21,045
  - Job 102: $10,000 materials + $1,728 labor (72 × $24) + $1,080 overhead (72 × $15) = $12,808
- Universal job cost structure: Direct Materials + Direct Labor + Overhead = Total Job Cost
