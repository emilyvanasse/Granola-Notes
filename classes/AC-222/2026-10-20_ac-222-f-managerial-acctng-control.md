## Summary

# Budgeting Overview and Process

- Strategy cycle: develop strategy, plan, act, control (with feedback for corrective action)
- Starts with company mission/purpose statement
- Strategic planning: long-term goals, 5-10 years out
- Short-term budgets designed to attain strategic goals
- Rolling budget: continually updated so next 12 months are always covered

# Budgeting Approaches

- Participative budgeting: involves all management levels
  - Lower-level managers closer to operations, more motivated by budgets they help create
  - More complex and time-consuming
- Zero-based budgeting: managers start from zero and must justify every dollar
- Budget committee uses prior year actuals or estimates as starting point
- Benchmarking: set targets at a “sweet spot,” not too easy or too discouraging

# Types of Budgets

- Operating budgets: run daily operations, culminate in the budgeted income statement
  - Sales budget (starting point for everything)
  - Production budget (manufacturer only)
  - Direct materials, direct labor, overhead budgets
  - Operating expenses budget
- Financial budgets: culminate in the budgeted balance sheet
  - Capital expenditures (large purchases: buildings, equipment)
  - Cash collections and cash payments budgets
  - Combined cash budget

# Tucson Tortilla: Operating Budget Walkthrough

- Sales budget: 30,000 cases in Jan at $20/case = $600,000; Feb 20,000 = $400,000; Mar 25,000 = $500,000
  - 20% cash, 80% credit each month
- Production budget formula: units needed for sales + desired ending inventory - beginning inventory
  - Ending inventory = 10% of next month’s sales
  - Jan: 30,000 + 2,000 - 3,000 = 29,000 cases to produce
- Direct materials: 5 lbs/case at $1.50/lb (calculations likely provided on exam)
- Direct labor: 0.05 hrs/case at $22/hr; Jan = 1,450 hrs = $31,900
- Overhead:
  - Variable: indirect materials $1.25/case, indirect labor $0.75/case, utilities $0.50/case
  - Fixed: $30,000/month regardless of production volume (depreciation, insurance, supervisors, base utilities)
  - Jan total overhead: $102,500
- Budgeted income statement: sales - COGS ($12/case absorption) - operating expenses = operating income
  - Jan: $600,000 - $360,000 - $142,800 = $97,200 operating income
  - Corporate tax rate: 21% flat

# Tucson Tortilla: Financial Budget Walkthrough

- Capital expenditures: all in January ($15K computers/printers, $35K vans, $75K equipment = $125,000)
- Cash collections: cash sales + 85% of prior month’s credit sales + 14% of two-months-prior credit sales
  - 1% of credit sales treated as bad debt (never collected)
  - Jan total collections: $612,200
- Cash payments: materials paid 1 month after purchase; labor paid within the month
  - Depreciation and monthly property tax/insurance accruals excluded; semiannual payments added in Jan and Jul
  - Jan total payments: $653,245 (includes $125K capex + $25K dividend)
- Combined cash budget:
  - Jan: $36,100 opening + $612,200 collections - $653,245 payments = deficit; borrowed $20,000 to reach $15,055
  - Feb: repaid $20,150 (principal + interest), ending balance $153,980
  - Quarter-end balance: $211,510

# Budgets by Company Type

- Manufacturer: full budget suite (sales, production, materials, labor, overhead, opex, income statement, capex, cash, balance sheet)
- Merchandiser: no production budget; replaces with cost of goods sold/inventory purchases budget
  - Formula: COGS + desired ending inventory - beginning inventory = purchases needed
  - Circle J example: $500K Jan sales, 60% COGS, 10% ending inventory of next month’s COGS
- Service company: no inventory; sales/revenue budget + operating expenses + financial budgets only
