## Summary

### Velocity and the Quantity Theory of Money

- FRED (Federal Reserve Economic Database) recommended for macro data
- Velocity reasonably stable pre-1990, ranging \~1.65–1.80
- Rose sharply after 1990, then fell during dot-com bust, GFC, and COVID
  - COVID drop: \~18% decrease, but rebounded quickly
- Percentage change in velocity fluctuates around zero over time
  - Assuming zero growth rate is imperfect but reasonable over any 10-year window

### Fisher Equation

- Real interest rate = nominal rate minus inflation
- Looking backward: real return is certain (e.g., 10% nominal minus 3% inflation = 7% real)
- Looking forward: uses expected inflation, since future is unknown
  - Expected real rate = nominal rate minus E(inflation)
- If actual inflation diverges from expected, realized real rate differs from expected real rate
- Key implication: unpredictable inflation makes borrowing/lending costs unclear
  - Lenders and borrowers can’t assess profit/loss, so markets shut down
  - Thin capital markets hurt real estate development, small businesses, etc.
- US inflation target: 2% (Federal Reserve); above target since COVID, trending back down

### Costs of Inflation

- **Financial intermediation breakdown**: unpredictable inflation kills lending/borrowing markets
- **Price signal confusion**: rising prices from inflation look like rising demand
  - Business owners may wrongly increase supply, hire staff, order ingredients
  - Leads to misallocation and firm failures
- **Money illusion**: people think in nominal terms, not real terms
  - Example: 10% raise with 15% inflation = 5% real pay cut
  - Only becomes a practical problem when inflation is high enough to matter
- **Menu cost**: cost of updating prices throughout the economy
  - Small at 2%; significant at higher rates
  - Firms often hold prices then jump, rather than adjust continuously
- **Shoe leather cost**: cost of avoiding holding depreciating cash
  - Hyperinflation example: workers sprint to spend wages before purchasing power erodes
  - At moderate inflation: fees from moving money in/out of investment accounts
- **Emotional bargaining cost** (newer research): people avoid asking for raises even at 4% real wage loss
  - Most workers would sacrifice 4% of salary to avoid a hard conversation with their boss
  - Explains persistent public anger even as inflation fell back toward 2%

### Inflation Terminology

- **Inflation**: average prices rising
- **Hyperinflation**: prices rising 50%+ per month
  - Examples: Zimbabwe (prices doubled daily at peak), Weimar Republic, Argentina
  - Caused by excessive money printing; ends in loss of confidence and often regime change
  - Remedies: official dollarization (Ecuador), unofficial dollarization (Zimbabwe), currency board (Lithuania pegged to German mark)
- **Deflation**: negative inflation, prices falling
- **Disinflation**: inflation slowing but still positive
  - US post-COVID: disinflation, not deflation

### Labor Market Indicators

- Starting population: adult (16+), civilian, non-institutionalized (excludes children, military, incarcerated, hospitalized)
- Two top-level groups:
  - Not in the labor force (NILF): no job, not looking
  - Labor force (LF): employed + unemployed
- Unemployed = no job AND actively looking within the last 4 weeks
  - Measured via Current Population Survey (CPS): \~160,000 households surveyed monthly by Bureau of Labor Statistics

**Three key indicators:**

- **Unemployment rate (UR)** = Unemployed / Labor Force
- **Labor force participation rate (LFPR)** = Labor Force / Adult population
- **EPOP** = Employed / Adult population

**Why multiple measures matter:**

- Discouraged worker leaves labor force: UR improves, LFPR worsens
- New entrant starts job-searching: UR rises, LFPR rises
- EPOP is simpler but still imperfect on its own
- Prime-age EPOP (ages 25–55) strips out students and retirees for a cleaner read

**Practice example** (A = 200, Employed = 120, NILF = 70):

- Unemployed = 200 − 120 − 70 = 10
- UR = 10/130 = 7.69%
- LFPR = 130/200 = 65%
- EPOP = 120/200 = 60%
