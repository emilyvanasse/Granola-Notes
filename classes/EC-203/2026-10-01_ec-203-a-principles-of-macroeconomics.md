## Summary

### GDP Deflator Recap

- GDP deflator = (Nominal GDP / Real GDP) × 100
  - Captures the ratio of current prices to base-year prices
  - Base year always equals 100; any value above 100 means prices have risen
- Worked example (Year 2 with Year 1 as base year):
  - Nominal GDP Year 2: 60×80 + 5×800 + 22×100 = 11,000
  - Real GDP Year 2: 40×80 + 4×800 + 20×100 = 8,400
  - GDP deflator: (11,000 / 8,400) × 100 = 130.95
- Percentage change in GDP deflator = first measure of inflation
- Two distinct “×100” operations:
  - In a price index: sets base year = 100 (index units, not a percentage)
  - In a growth rate: converts a decimal to a percentage

### Real GDP Per Capita

- Real GDP per capita = Real GDP / Population
  - Adjusts for both inflation and population size
  - Enables cross-country and cross-time comparisons
- Strong proxy for standard of living
  - Highly correlated with life expectancy, infant mortality, job quality, education access, healthcare access, gender equality
  - US: \~$75K per capita; India: \~$12K per capita
  - Luxembourg: smaller total GDP than US, but higher GDP per capita
- Not a perfect measure, but the best available
- Population growth matters: if real GDP grows 2% but population grows 3%, real GDP per capita shrinks \~1%

### Drawbacks of GDP

- Does not capture non-priced goods and services
  - Household labor (cleaning, childcare): zero price, so zero contribution
  - Free digital services (Google Maps): estimated \~$250/month willingness-to-pay per adult, but not counted
- Does not capture unreported (underground) goods
  - Illegal drug sales: reported quantity is zero
  - Legalizing drugs can raise measured GDP without improving actual welfare
- Does not capture leisure
  - Spain example: long lunches, afternoon naps, shorter workdays = lower GDP but high life satisfaction
  - US high-work-ethic culture inflates GDP relative to Spain’s, but neither is objectively better

### Inflation: Definition and Measurement Framework

- Inflation = percentage change in the price level (growth rate of the price level)
- Price level = a measure of average prices across the economy
- Key distinction: relative price change vs. inflation
  - Egg prices rising alone (e.g., due to bird flu) = relative price change, not inflation
  - Oil is a special case: relative price change that ripples across many goods and can mimic inflation
- Price index construction:
  - Fix a basket of goods with set quantities
  - Track basket price across years
  - Price Index = (Basket Price in Year T / Basket Price in Base Year) × 100
  - Base year always = 100
- Snack basket worked example (base year 2022):
  - 2022 basket price: $40 → SPI = 100
  - 2023 basket price: $54 → SPI = 135
  - Inflation = (135 − 100) / 100 × 100 = **35%**
- Inflation is a weighted average of individual price changes
  - Popcorn: 50% price increase, 25% spending share → 12.5 pp contribution
  - Popcorners: 25% price increase, 60% spending share → 15 pp contribution
  - St. Andre: 50% price increase, 15% spending share → 7.5 pp contribution
  - Total: 35 percentage points

### Price Index Types and Current Data

- Three main price indexes (differ only in what goes in the basket):
  - GDP Deflator: broadest basket, all goods and services in GDP (includes tractors, etc.)
  - CPI (Consumer Price Index): basket of goods consumers actually buy; most widely reported
  - PCE (Personal Consumption Expenditure): based on the “C” in GDP = C + I + G + NX; preferred by the Federal Reserve
- Core CPI / Core PCE = headline measure excluding food and energy
  - Food and energy prices are highly volatile, adding noise
  - Core strips out volatility to reveal the underlying trend
- Current data (released the day of class):
  - Headline PCE: 3.4% annual inflation
  - Core PCE: 3.0%
  - Food and energy prices currently running above the overall average
