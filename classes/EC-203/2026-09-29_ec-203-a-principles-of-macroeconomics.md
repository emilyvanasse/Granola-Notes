## Summary

### Nominal vs. Real GDP (Review)

- Nominal GDP: prices x quantities, both from the year being measured (inflation included)
- Real GDP: base-year prices x current-year quantities (inflation removed)
- In the base year, nominal GDP = real GDP
- Summation notation (Σ PᵢQᵢ) compresses the formula but doesn’t change the calculation
- Example from last class:
  - Nominal GDP: $10,000 (yr 1), $11,000 (yr 2)
  - Real GDP: $10,000 (yr 1), $8,400 (yr 2); nominal grew, real shrank

### GDP as Output, Spending, and Income

- GDP measures three things simultaneously: production, spending, and income
- Hoodie example: value added = final sale price = total factor income = $40
  - Cotton grower: $5 value added ($2.50 wages, $1 rent, $1.50 profit)
  - Fabric mill: $10 value added ($7.50 wages, $1 rent, $1.50 profit)
  - Hoodie maker: $25 value added ($15 wages, $1 rent, $9 profit)
- Income expenditure approach: employee compensation + rent + interest + profit = GDP
- BEA calculates GDP quarterly via the spending approach; Y = C + I + G + NX

### The Four Components of GDP

- **C: Personal Consumption Expenditure**

  - Durable goods (washing machine, car): expected to last many years
  - Non-durable goods (food, coffee): used up quickly
  - Services (concerts, haircuts, tuition)

- **I: Gross Private Domestic Investment** (not stocks/bonds)

  - Capital purchases: firms buying equipment to produce more
  - Inventory: goods produced but not yet sold
    - Prevents double-counting when goods sell in a later quarter
  - New residential construction counts as I, not C
  - Rule of thumb: firm spending = I; household spending = C

- **G: Government Purchases**

  - All levels of government (federal, state, local)
  - Only counts when government directly buys goods/services
  - Transfer payments do NOT count in G (SNAP, unemployment insurance, Social Security, Medicare, Medicaid, stimulus checks)
    - These show up under C when the recipient spends them

- **NX: Net Exports (X − M)**

  - Exports: US-produced goods sold abroad, captured here
  - Imports subtracted to remove foreign-produced goods already counted in C, I, or G
  - Higher imports don’t reduce GDP on net: the import is added elsewhere and then subtracted out (Nutella example)
  - US net exports currently negative (−$0.876T, −2.7% of GDP)

### GDP Growth Rate and the GDP Deflator

- Growth rate formula: (new − old) / old × 100
  - Positive = economy grew; negative = economy shrank
  - Growing slower ≠ shrinking; only a negative rate means contraction
- Example series (real GDP): 100, 104, 106, 103, 105
  - Yr 1→2: +4%; Yr 2→3: +1.92%; Yr 3→4: −2.83%; Yr 4→5: +1.94%
- GDP Deflator = (Nominal GDP / Real GDP) × 100
  - Always equals 100 in the base year
  - Above 100: prices higher than base year; below 100: prices lower
  - Inflation rate = % change in GDP deflator (new − old / old)
