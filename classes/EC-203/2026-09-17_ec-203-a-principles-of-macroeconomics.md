### Supply and Demand: Key Concepts Covered

- Shortages create upward price pressure, moving along the supply curve until equilibrium
- Surpluses create downward price pressure (discounts, sales) to clear inventory
- Price changes move us along the curve; input cost changes shift the curve
  - Example: rising spare parts costs shift the bicycle repair supply curve left, creating a shortage at the old price
- Consumer surplus: area below the demand curve and above the price
- Producer surplus: area above the supply curve and below the price

### International Trade and World Price

- Domestic market vs. world market: if world price is lower, consumers prefer imports
  - Example: cheaper foreign coffee pulls demand away from domestic suppliers (e.g., Starbucks)
- At the world price, domestic quantity supplied and quantity demanded diverge
  - Gap between domestic supply and domestic demand is filled by imports
  - No shortage or surplus: consumers are fully satisfied at the world price
- Domestic producers only supply up to the quantity where their marginal cost equals the world price
  - Above that quantity, they can't compete and won't produce
- Tariff effect: raises the effective price above the world price
  - Domestic producers willing to supply more at the higher price
  - Consumers buy less; import volume shrinks
- Consumer surplus with world price: larger triangle (price falls, quantity demanded rises)
  - Example calculation: ½ × (6 − 2) × 80 = 160
