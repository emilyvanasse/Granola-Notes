## Summary

### AS-AD Model Review

- Axes: inflation (Y), growth rate of real output (X)
- Aggregate Demand (AD): downward sloping, equals growth of money + growth of velocity (fixed nominal spending)
- Long Run Aggregate Supply (LRAS): vertical, at potential growth rate where unemployment = natural rate
- Short Run Aggregate Supply (SRAS): upward sloping, expected inflation = actual inflation where LRAS and SRAS cross
- Equilibrium: intersection of AD and SRAS; long run equilibrium when also on LRAS

### What Shifts Each Curve

- AD: change in growth rate of money or velocity (C, I, G, or NX)
- LRAS: change in resources, technology, or institutions
- SRAS: change in inflation expectations, or anytime LRAS shifts (short run is contained within the long run)

### Practice Problems: Key Concepts Worked Through

- Nominal spending fixed at 8%: if inflation rises (3% to 5%), real output growth falls proportionally
  - Intuition: same nominal spend can’t support as much real growth when prices rise
  - Coffee shop analogy: output price rises but input (labor) costs haven’t, so real wages fall in relative terms
  - This is movement along SRAS, not a shift, because inflation expectations haven’t changed
- Equilibrium always sits at intersection of AD and SRAS, not SRAS alone
  - Knowing the SRAS curve exists (or its position) is not enough to determine where the economy is

### Velocity vs. Money: Temporary vs. Permanent Shifts

- Change in growth rate of money: permanent shift, economy stays at new equilibrium long enough for inflation expectations to adjust
- Change in velocity (e.g., investment drop): temporary, velocity returns to zero over long run
  - AD shifts out then back; SRAS does not shift (expectations don’t have time to adjust)
  - Economy moves to new short run equilibrium, then returns when velocity normalizes
- Key distinction between problems 1 and 2: money change (permanent) vs. velocity change (temporary)
