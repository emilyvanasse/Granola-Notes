## Summary

### GDP Definition and Scale

- GDP = Gross Domestic Product: market value of all final goods and services produced within an economy in a year
- Current US GDP: $32.5 trillion (Q2 2026, annualized)
- Scale visualization: $100 bill → $10K stack → $1M briefcase → $1B school bus → $1T MetLife field → $32T overfills the stadium

### Breaking Down the Definition

- **Market value:** price × quantity, converts all goods/services into common units (dollars/year)
- **Final only:** no double-counting intermediate goods
  - Cotton ($5) → fabric ($15) → hoodie ($40): only the $40 counts
  - Value-added at each stage sums to the same final figure
- **Produced:** transfers don’t count (gifts, stock purchases, used goods resales)
  - Used laptop sold for $100: not counted; $50 repair service: counted
- **Within a year:** GDP is a flow, not a stock; measured quarterly but annualized
  - Stock vs. flow: comparing personal wealth (stock) to a country’s GDP (flow) is a category error
- **In an economy:** where it was made matters, not who made it
  - Indonesian T-shirt: adds to Indonesian GDP, not US
  - Peruvian immigrant cooking in the US: counts toward US GDP

### Goods vs. Services

- US GDP split: \~65% services, \~35% goods
- Higher-income countries tend toward services; developing economies toward goods
  - US strengths: financial services, healthcare
  - Bangladesh example: goods-focused (clothing manufacturing)
- Services = experiences (education, healthcare, concerts, escape rooms)

### Nominal vs. Real GDP (Simple Economy Example)

Three-good economy: Sacred Heart hoodies, cold brew cans, escape room visits

Year 1 Year 2 Hoodies 100 @ $40 80 @ $60 Cold brew 1,000 @ $4 800 @ $5 Escape rooms 100 @ $20 100 @ $22 **Nominal GDP** **$10,000** **$11,000**

- Nominal GDP rose despite producing less: prices increased (inflation)
- **Nominal:** includes inflation; **Real:** removes inflation (mnemonic: N includes, R removes)

### Real GDP Calculation

- Fix prices at base year (Year 1); apply to quantities in each year
  - Real GDP Year 1: $10,000 (same as nominal; base year prices = current prices)
  - Real GDP Year 2: (80×$40) + (800×$4) + (100×$20) = **$8,400**
- Real GDP fell: production actually declined once inflation is stripped out
- Nominal vs. real distinction applies beyond GDP: interest rates, wages, and more
