## Summary

### Model Overview: Aggregate Supply and Aggregate Demand

- AS-AD model used to evaluate short-run economic fluctuations (business cycle)
- Also used to assess fiscal policy (Congress) and monetary policy (Fed) responses
- Axes: inflation (Y), growth rate of real GDP (X), both in growth rates
- Two key time periods:
  - Short run: not all prices can adjust (e.g., rent, tuition locked in by contract)
  - Long run: all prices perfectly flexible

### Aggregate Demand Curve

- Downward sloping; each AD curve fixes the growth rate of nominal spending
  - Nominal spending = growth rate of money (gM) + growth rate of velocity (gV)
  - As inflation rises, real output growth must fall (and vice versa) to keep nominal spending fixed
- Movement along the curve: change in inflation or change in real output growth
- Curve shifts when nominal spending changes:
  - gM changes: controlled by the Fed (Federal Reserve)
    - “Fed” is not an acronym: lowercase f-e-d, not FED
  - gV changes: driven by changes in C, I, G, or NX
    - Consumers lose confidence → less consumption → gV falls → AD shifts left
    - Firms expect recession → less investment → AD shifts left
    - Dollar appreciates → imports rise, exports fall → NX falls → AD shifts left
    - Government increases spending → AD shifts right

### Long-Run Aggregate Supply (LRAS) Curve

- Vertical at the long-run potential growth rate (GY\*), currently \~2.5% for the US
- Long-run real output determined solely by real factors, not prices:
  - Resources (land, labor, capital)
  - Technology (productivity of capital)
  - Institutions (property rights, rule of law, stable government)
- Movement along the curve: change in inflation only
- Curve shifts when GY\* changes (i.e., resources, technology, or institutions change)

### AD + LRAS Equilibrium and Unemployment

- Equilibrium where AD and LRAS intersect: pins down both inflation and real output growth
- Unemployment relative to natural rate:
  - Real output growth > GY\*: unemployment below natural rate (cyclical unemployment negative)
  - Real output growth = GY\*: unemployment equals natural rate
  - Real output growth < GY\*: unemployment above natural rate
- Example A: technology increases
  - LRAS shifts right → higher output growth, lower inflation
  - Unemployment still equals natural rate, but natural rate itself falls
- Example B: consumers lose confidence
  - AD shifts left → lower inflation, output growth unchanged
  - Unemployment unchanged: prices absorb the full impact in the long run

### Real Business Cycle Theory and the Road Ahead

- RBC theory (Kydland and Prescott, Nobel 2004): business cycle driven by changes in real factors only
  - Prices perfectly flexible → demand-side shifts have no output or unemployment effect
  - Limitation: doesn’t capture reality well (prices are sticky in practice)
- All changes to gV are temporary by assumption: velocity mean-reverts to zero in the long run
  - AD shifts from velocity changes will eventually reverse themselves
- Next class: short-run aggregate supply (SRAS) curve, relaxing the flexible-price assumption
