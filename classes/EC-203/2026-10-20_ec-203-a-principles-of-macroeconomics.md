## Summary

### The Loanable Funds Market: Overview

- Savings and investment connect through the market for loanable funds
- Households are the suppliers (savers); firms are the demanders (borrowers)
- Equilibrium interest rate set where savings supply meets investment demand
- Upward-sloping supply curve (savings), downward-sloping demand curve (investment)

### Time Preference and the Save-or-Spend Decision

- Any income has exactly two uses: consume now or save (delay consumption)
- Time preference: the tendency to prefer goods/consumption now over later
  - High time preference: impatient, more likely to spend
  - Low time preference: patient, more likely to save
- Time preference has fallen over centuries as life expectancy has risen
  - Historical example: Calvin Coolidge’s son died from a blister (pre-antibiotics)
  - Short life expectancy made the future less worth waiting for
  - College attendance as modern evidence: delaying consumption for future earnings

### Investment Decisions and the Interest Rate

- Firms borrow to fund investment (inventory, equipment, new locations) before earning revenue
- Decision rule: borrow only if expected return exceeds the interest rate
  - Example: borrow $100k at 5%, shop earns $101k (1% return) = losing money
  - Need return ≥ interest rate just to break even
- As real interest rate rises, firms borrow less (fewer projects are profitable)
- As real interest rate falls, more projects become viable, borrowing increases

### Shifting the Curves

- Supply of loanable funds (savings) shifts when:
  - Households more worried about the future (save more)
  - Big anticipated purchases ahead (save more now to dis-save later)
  - Time preference falls (more patient, save more)
- Demand for loanable funds (investment) shifts when:
  - Economic outlook improves (more borrowing to invest)
  - Expected profitability of projects rises
  - New technology emerges (e.g., AI, data centers, fast-pizza ovens) → more investment opportunities
- Key distinction: interest rate changes move along the curve; everything else shifts it
