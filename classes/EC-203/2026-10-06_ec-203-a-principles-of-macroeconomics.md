## Summary

### Equation of Exchange

- Core identity: M × V = P × Y (money × velocity = price level × real output)
  - Accounting identity, not a theory: true by construction
  - Rearranged: P = (M × V) / Y
- In growth rates: gM + gV = gP + gY
  - Times become pluses (approximation of the full derivation)
  - gP (growth rate of price level) = inflation
- Price level vs. inflation
  - Price level: point-in-time average (CPI, PCE, GDP deflator)
  - Inflation: growth rate of the price level over time

### Quantity Theory of Money (QTM)

- Two assumptions added to the equation of exchange:
  1. Velocity growth rate is stable (approximately zero)
  2. Real output determined by real factors outside the model
- Real factors driving output: resources (land, labor, capital), technology, institutions
  - More dollars don’t make more cheeseburgers; you need beef, wheat, labor
- With gV ≈ 0 and gY fixed externally, inflation is driven by gM
  - “If money grows faster than the real economy, prices rise”
- Associated with Milton Friedman; Nobel Prize winner, dominant economist of the 1970s–80s
- Policy relevance
  - Pre-1985: Fed targeted money supply directly
  - Post-1990: Fed switched to interest rates as the policy lever
  - New Fed chair Kevin Warsh reportedly reviving QTM-based thinking

### Fisher Effect and Real vs. Nominal Interest Rates

- Fisher equation: r = i − π (real rate = nominal rate − inflation)
- Example: $100 borrowed at 5% nominal, 2% expected inflation
  - Expected real rate: 5% − 2% = 3%
  - Lender expects to receive \~$103 in real terms
- Unexpected inflation (actual 8% vs. expected 2%)
  - Real value of $105 repayment ≈ $97.20
  - Actual real rate: 5% − 8% = −3%
  - Borrower wins: repays in cheaper dollars
  - Lender loses: receives less purchasing power than expected
- Fisher Effect: unexpected inflation redistributes wealth from lenders to borrowers
  - Reverse holds if inflation undershoots expectations
- Stable, predictable inflation matters for functioning credit markets
  - Persistent surprises cause lenders or borrowers to exit the market

### Key Takeaways to Know

- Equation of exchange: M × V = P × Y and its growth-rate form
- QTM’s two assumptions and what they imply for inflation
- Fisher equation: r = i − π, and how unexpected inflation shifts real returns
