### Tariffs and Trade (Problem Set 1, Q9)

- Setup: Qd = 160 − 20P, Qs = 20P, world price = $2; $1 tariff raises price to $3
- Key table (free trade vs. tariff):
  - Domestic price: $2 → $3
  - Domestic production (Qs): 40 → 60
  - Domestic consumption (Qd): 120 → 100
  - Imports: 80 → 40 (fall of 40, split evenly: +20 domestic production, −20 consumption)
- Surplus table:
  - Consumer surplus: 360 → 250
  - Producer surplus: 40 → 90
  - Government revenue: 0 → 40 (tariff × imports = $1 × 40)
  - Total surplus (incl. govt revenue): 400 → 380; deadweight loss = 20
- Tariff incidence: falls entirely on domestic consumers (world supply is perfectly elastic)
- Marginal producer at Q=50: costs $2.50 to produce, charges $3, but import price is $2 → 50¢ efficiency loss
- Marginal buyer at Q=110: WTP $2.50, must pay $3 → priced out; would gain 50¢ surplus under free trade
- Production and consumption losses need not be equal: depends on relative slopes of supply and demand curves

### Comparative Advantage and Trade Ratios

- Framework: two goods (A, B), two countries (C, D)
  - Example: C produces 10A or 20B; D produces 4A or 6B
- Opportunity costs (in terms of the other good):
  - C: 1A costs 2B; 1B costs 0.5A
  - D: 1A costs 1.5B; 1B costs 0.67A
- Absolute advantage: C dominates in both goods
- Comparative advantage: D in good A (lower opp. cost: 1.5 < 2); C in good B (0.5 < 0.67)
- Trade ratio for 1 unit of A: between 1.5 and 2 units of B (between each country’s opportunity costs)
  - Scales linearly: 2A trades for 3–4B
- PPF equations (e.g., G = 120 − 2W) are just lines through the production endpoints; plotting endpoints gives the same result

### Key Equations and Concepts for the Exam

- **GDP:** Y = C + I + G + (X − M)

  - Imports appear in both C and M (net effect = 0 if added; replacing domestic with imports reduces GDP)
  - Inventory = investment; used goods and transfer payments excluded
  - Value added = final sale price; wages + profits = same production, not additional GDP

- **Fisher equation:** Nominal rate = Real rate + Inflation

  - Nominal rate is fixed in contracts; real rate depends on actual vs. expected inflation
  - Higher-than-expected inflation → lower real rate → benefits borrowers, hurts lenders

- **Equation of exchange:** M × V = P × Y (or in growth rates: ṁ + v̇ = π + ẏ)

  - Example: money growth 5%, velocity stable, real GDP growth 3% → inflation = 2%
  - GDP deflator = (Nominal GDP / Real GDP) × 100; growth rate of deflator = inflation

- **Labor market formulas:**

  - Unemployment rate = Unemployed / Labor force
  - Labor force participation rate = Labor force / Adult civilian non-institutionalized population (ACNP)
  - Employment-population ratio (EPOP) = Employed / ACNP
  - Labor market tightness = Job openings / Unemployed
  - Natural rate = frictional + structural unemployment (zero cyclical unemployment)
    - Output below potential → unemployment above natural rate (positive cyclical)
    - Output above potential → unemployment below natural rate (negative cyclical)
